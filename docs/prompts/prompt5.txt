1. LEARNING FIRST: HOW A LEARNING CARD DIFFERS FROM A TEST QUESTION
================================================================================
Before writing a card, finish this sentence: "After reading this, the employee can ______." If you cannot, do not write the card.

Rules that make a card teach instead of test:
  1. ANSWER FIRST. The first line of the body states the idea or the answer. The rest explains it. Never make the reader guess before they learn.
  2. TERM FIRST. Every business term (free-of-cost quantity, challan, tolerance, drop-shipment, Release status) is defined in plain words the first time it appears on the card. A new employee must be able to read the card cold. If a card only makes sense after another card, rewrite it or drop it.
  3. SELF-CONTAINED SCENARIOS. Put every number and condition the reader needs inside the card. Never write "the tolerance warning from before" or "as taught earlier".
  4. WHY, NOT JUST WHAT. Each medium and hard card explains the business reason behind the rule.
  5. BEHAVIOUR, NOT LABELS. Teach what the system does and what the user should do. Never teach an internal rule id, message id, code number or column name. Teach the behaviour behind it. ("An authorized receipt cannot be saved again" is the lesson, not the rule's number.)
  6. NO EXAM WORDING in the body: no "Question:", "Answer:", "Name three", "True or false", "Which of the following".
  7. ONE IDEA PER CARD. If a card needs "and also", split it.

The title is the front of the card: a question or claim the employee can self-check ("Do I know this?"). The body is the back: the lesson.

EASY ARCHETYPES (what, who, when)
  E1  One-line definition: what the thing records or does, and what it is NOT.
      angle: purpose.   Example: "What does a Material Receipt Note record, and what does it not do?"
  E2  Look-alike terms told apart: two words people confuse, measured against different things.
      angle: vocabulary.   Example: "Partial receipt vs short receipt: which is which?" / "Purchase Order vs receipt: promise vs proof."
  E3  Where it sits in the flow: what comes before, what it hands over, what it feeds.
      angle: position-in-flow.   Example: "Why is there no direct link from a receipt to Accounts Payable? It goes through the Purchase Bill."
  E4  Term first: one business term that later cards rely on, defined with a plain example.
      angle: vocabulary.   Example: "What is free-of-cost quantity and why is it kept apart?" / "What is a drop-shipment receipt?" / "What does GR Suspense hold?"
  E5  Takeaway or common slip in one line: a mistake learners make, stated and corrected.
      angle: purpose or outputs.   Example: "Why must you never treat the challan quantity as the received quantity?"

MEDIUM ARCHETYPES (how and why)
  M1  Rule with its reason: the rule, the business reason, the user's fix.
      angle: standard-checks.   Example: "Why can an authorized receipt not be saved again?" / "Why must the source document be chosen first?"
  M2  Steps in order, with why the order matters.
      angle: sequence.   Example: "What are the steps to receive material against an order, and what happens on save?"
  M3  Formula with a small worked example.
      angle: limits-tolerances.   Example: "How are accepted, short and excess quantity worked out?" (include numbers)
  M4  Route A versus route B: what one path adds or locks compared with the other.
      angle: prerequisites or hand-offs.   Example: "What does receiving through a gate entry lock compared with receiving against an order?"
  M5  Status meaning: what each status allows and what moves it on.
      angle: status-lifecycle.   Example: "What can still be changed at each status, and what is locked after inspection?"
  M6  Who must do what: roles, approval order, what only the last approver can do.
      angle: approvals-roles.   Example: "Under multi-level approval, who can authorize and where?"
  M7  Misconception: "Why did X NOT happen?" or "Why is the common belief wrong?"
      angle: standard-checks or status-lifecycle.   Example: "Why did stock not rise when stores saved the receipt?" / "A form field looks compulsory on screen: is it really required?"

HARD ARCHETYPES (apply and consequences)
  H1  Numeric scenario: apply a rule to figures and state the decision and the result.
      angle: tolerance-calculation.   Example: "Order 2,000, tolerance 2 percent, three deliveries: is the third allowed and what is the excess?" / "Challan 1,000, counted 985, rejected 15: what goes to supplier, stores and accounts?"
  H2  Cascade: a change or event here, and every record it moves elsewhere.
      angle: cascade-effects or cross-module-impact.   Example: "What happens to other records when an authorized receipt is deleted?" / "What changes downstream when freight is added after the receipt?"
  H3  Diagnose: a symptom, the likely causes, the order to check them.
      angle: failure-diagnosis.   Example: "An authorized receipt shows no stock: what to check, in order." / "A reference does not appear in the list: likely causes."
  H4  Correction and reversal: what to do when a posted document is wrong.
      angle: reversal-correction.   Example: "A wrong receipt is authorized and part of its stock is already used: what are the options?"
  H5  Case walkthrough: one realistic case, step by step, through several controls.
      angle: scenario-walkthrough.   Example: "Three deliveries of steel plate against one order, with inspection and a month-end in between."
  H6  Conflicting controls or exceptions: two checks that seem to disagree.
      angle: exceptions.   Example: "The user accepts a warning but the save is still refused: why?"

ARCHETYPES THAT MUST NOT BECOME CARDS (they test memory, they do not teach)
  X1  Identifier lookup: "Which message appears when...", "Which column holds...", "Which validation number...", "About how many forms use...". Teach the behaviour, never the label.
  X2  Keyboard shortcuts and hidden keys.
  X3  Technical structure for a business audience: table names, column names, procedure order, save-transaction internals.
  X4  Long list recall: "List all seven causes". Write one card per cause, or a diagnosis card (H3) that gives the checks in order.
  X5  Open points: anything the source marks as not documented, inferred, or to be confirmed. Skip and mention it in your reply message.
  X6  Questions that need another card or session to make sense.

Common archetypes (each follows the level rules of section 2; the level shown is the usual one)
  P1  Shared rule: a rule that holds across several forms, with the reason.   medium   angle: standard-checks or approvals-roles
      Example: "Why is an authorized document locked, and how is it changed?" / "Why is a master record made inactive instead of deleted once it is used?"
  P2  Two forms compared: what each one records and who owns it.   easy or medium   angle: vocabulary or position-in-flow
      Example: "What is the difference between a gate entry and a material receipt?"
  P3  Hand-off chain: what each step needs from the one before and what blocks the next.   medium   angle: hand-offs or sequence
      Example: "What must exist before a purchase bill can be passed, and where does it come from?"
  P4  Cross-form scenario: one case followed through several documents.   hard   angle: scenario-walkthrough or cascade-effects
      Example: "A supplier delivers short: what happens at receipt, to the bill and to the order?"
  P5  Business idea used in many places: define it once, show where it applies.   easy or medium   angle: vocabulary or purpose
      Example: "What is tolerance, and which documents check it?"

How to write them safely
  - EVIDENCE. State a pattern only if the source shows it on at least three of the forms you name (two for a comparison of two forms). Never generalise from one form. If the forms differ, write the difference (P2) or skip the card.
  - SOURCE_REFS. List the form files the pattern was checked against. They record where the pattern was checked and link the card to those forms in the knowledge base.
  - NAMES. Use business names only, never form codes (the leak check rejects them).
  - ITEMS. Use scope "flow" for patterns, flows and business ideas, and scope "module" for a module overview (key = the module code, for example MM). For a flow that already has a process file, use that process key (for example procure-to-pay). For a pattern that has no file, make a key from the module code, the topic and the word common, in lowercase with hyphens: mm-transaction-forms-common, mm-master-forms-common, mm-lot-concept-common. Item title: the business name, for example "Transaction Forms: Common Questions". Context: "Common patterns > Transaction forms".
  - MIX. About 70 percent form cards, 15 percent common cards, 15 percent flow and module cards, unless TARGET ITEMS says otherwise.
  - Common cards count in the ledger like any other: compare their titles with EXISTING CARDS of the same item.

CARD E1
level: easy
topic_key: purpose--what-a-receipt-records
title: What does a Material Receipt Note record, and what is it not?
body:
  A **Material Receipt Note** records what physically arrived from a supplier and how much was accepted.
  - It splits the delivery into **accepted**, **rejected** and **free** quantities.
  **Watch out:** it is proof of goods received, not the supplier's bill.
----
CARD E2
level: easy
topic_key: vocabulary--partial-versus-short-receipt
title: Partial receipt versus short receipt: which is which?
body:
  A **partial receipt** and a **short receipt** differ in what they are measured against.
  - **Partial:** less than the order arrives, and the challan agrees.
  - **Short:** the challan claims more than the stores counted.
  **Watch out:** a partial delivery with a correct challan is not short.
----
CARD E4
level: easy
topic_key: vocabulary--free-of-cost-quantity
title: What is free-of-cost quantity, and why is it kept apart on a receipt?
body:
  **Free-of-cost quantity** is material the supplier sends without charge, such as a sample.
  - It enters stock like any other quantity.
  - It is kept apart from the **paid quantity**, so it is not billed.
  **Watch out:** Finance can ask for a rate, so it is valued.
----
CARD M1
level: medium
topic_key: standard-checks--authorized-receipt-is-frozen
title: Why can an authorized receipt not be saved again?
body:
  Once a receipt is **Authorized**, the stock it created is live, so the document is frozen.
  **How it works:**
  - Saving a receipt as **Initial** or **Release** posts nothing to stock.
  - Authorizing posts the accepted quantity to stock in the same save.
  - After that the receipt cannot be saved again, because other documents may already depend on that stock.
  **Why it matters:** a silent edit could change stock that has already been issued or billed.
  **Watch out:** an authorized receipt can be deleted only while none of its stock has been used. Otherwise escalate to your supervisor.
----
CARD M3
level: medium
topic_key: limits-tolerances--accepted-short-excess-formulas
title: How are accepted, short and excess quantity worked out on a receipt?
body:
  The receipt works out three figures, so the storekeeper enters only what was counted.
  **How it works:**
  - **Accepted** = received minus rejected minus free-of-cost quantity (material sent without charge).
  - **Short** = challan quantity minus received quantity.
  - **Excess** = accepted plus already-received plus free quantity, minus the order quantity.
  **Why it matters:** short drives the supplier memo, excess drives the limit check, accepted drives stock and the bill.
  **Example:** received 300, free 20, rejected 0, rate ₹40: accepted is 280, amount ₹11,200.
  **Watch out:** excess is measured against the order, short against the challan.
----
CARD M7
level: medium
topic_key: status-lifecycle--saved-receipt-adds-no-stock
title: Why did stock not rise when stores saved the receipt?
body:
  A saved receipt does not always add stock. Stock rises only when the receipt reaches **Authorized**.
  **How it works:**
  - **Initial:** a draft, nothing is posted.
  - **Release:** ready for approval or inspection, still nothing is posted.
  - **Authorized:** the accepted quantity is added to stock in that same save.
  **Why it matters:** quality staff and approvers can check the receipt before the material counts as usable.
  **Watch out:** pending inspection or multi-level approval blocks authorization until those steps finish.
----
CARD H1
level: hard
topic_key: tolerance-calculation--third-delivery-against-one-order
title: Third delivery against one order: is it allowed, and what is the excess?
body:
  Deliveries against one order are checked **cumulatively**: each receipt must fit inside what is still open, plus the allowed **tolerance**.
  **Rule:**
  - Earlier receipts are added to the new quantity and compared with the order plus the tolerance percentage.
  - The screen warns on the quantity received. The final save refuses if the accepted quantity exceeds the open balance plus tolerance.
  **What it affects:** a refused receipt moves neither stock nor the supplier bill.
  **Worked example:**
  - Order 2,000 units, tolerance 2 percent, so the limit is 2,040. Earlier receipts were 800 and 690, so 1,490 is received.
  - Third delivery 540: the total is 2,030, below 2,040, so it is **allowed**. **Excess** = 2,030 minus 2,000 = 30 units.
  **If it goes wrong:** at 560 the total of 2,050 breaches the limit and the save is refused, even if the user answers Yes to the warning. Receive only 550, or ask Purchase to amend the order.
----
CARD H2
level: hard
topic_key: cascade-effects--deleting-an-authorized-receipt
title: What happens to other records when an authorized receipt is deleted?
body:
  Deleting an **authorized** receipt works only while none of its stock has been used. It is a hard removal, not a cancellation.
  **Rule:** there is no cancelled status and no reversal document, and delete is blocked once any of the receipt's stock is issued.
  **What it affects:**
  - The stock and the finance voucher raised by the receipt are removed.
  - The order, request and transfer lines it was received against go back to pending.
  - The deletion leaves no approval trail.
  **Worked example:** a receipt for 100 bearings was authorized this morning to the wrong warehouse. Nothing is issued, so it is deleted: the 100 leave stock and the order shows 100 pending. Had 10 been issued first, the delete would be refused.
  **If it goes wrong:** a receipt that cannot be deleted cannot be saved again either. Escalate to your supervisor or stock controller first.
----
CARD H3
level: hard
topic_key: failure-diagnosis--authorized-receipt-shows-no-stock
title: An authorized receipt shows no stock: what should you check, in order?
body:
  When an **authorized** receipt shows no stock in a report, the cause is usually by design, not an error. Check the simple explanations first.
  **What to check, in order:**
  - Receipt type: a drop-shipment receipt (goods go straight to the customer) posts no stock.
  - Quantities: if everything was rejected, the accepted quantity is zero, and rejected stock is kept apart.
  - Reservation: accepted stock may be reserved for a request, so it does not show as free stock.
  - Location: the stock may sit in a different warehouse or unit from the one the report covers.
  - Status: a Release receipt posts nothing.
  **Worked example:** a storekeeper authorized a receipt for 50 pumps for the second warehouse, and 20 were rejected. The main store report shows none, but 30 are in the second warehouse and 20 in rejected stock. Nothing is missing.
  **If it goes wrong:** if none of these explains it, escalate to the support team with the receipt number.
----
CARD P1 (a common card, not tied to one form: item scope "flow", see section 3B)
level: medium
topic_key: standard-checks--authorized-document-is-locked
title: Why is an authorized document locked, and how is it changed?
body:
  Most transaction documents in Material Management are **locked** once authorized, because stock, bills and other documents start to depend on them.
  **How it works:**
  - The last approver sets **Authorized**. From then on quantities, rates and references cannot be edited directly.
  - To change a purchase commitment, the user raises an **amendment**, which is approved again.
  - A stock document such as a receipt cannot be saved again, because its stock is already posted.
  **Why it matters:** a silent edit would break the link between the document and everything built on it.
  **Watch out:** locked is not final. Some documents can still be deleted, but only while nothing has used them.

5. ANSWER FORMAT (the "body")
================================================================================
The body is read on the back of the card. It must read like a good colleague explaining the topic: clear, structured, easy to scan.
  - Lines are separated by "\n" in the JSON string. One idea per line.
  - **Bold** (double asterisks) marks key business terms: roles, document names, statuses, limits, outcomes. Use 3-8 bold phrases per card, counting bold labels such as **Watch out:**. Each bold phrase is 1-4 words. Never bold a whole sentence. Never nest bold. Every ** closes on the same line.
  - Bullets start the line with "- " (dash and one space). No nested bullets. No numbered markup; for ordered steps write "- **Step 1:** ..." lines.
  - A line that is a bold label ending in a colon, e.g. "**How it works:**", introduces the bullets below it.
  - No headings (#), tables, links, emojis, italic markers or code formatting.
  - Plain sentences otherwise. Active voice, present tense. Short sentences. A SPACE after every full stop.

Shapes:
  EASY   : 1 lead line that defines the thing, then 1-3 bullets, optional "**Watch out:**".
  MEDIUM : 1 lead line, "**How it works:**" + 3-4 bullets, "**Why it matters:**" line, "**Watch out:**" line. For formulas use "**Example:**" in place of one bullet or the Watch out line, so the card stays within 8 lines.
  HARD   : 1 lead line, "**Rule:**" + bullets, "**What it affects:**" + bullets, "**Worked example:**" with real-looking numbers or a short scenario, "**If it goes wrong:**" with the consequence and the correct next step. A section may be a single line after its bold label. Stay within 12 lines. For diagnosis cards, use "**What to check, in order:**" in place of Rule.

Numbers in worked examples may be invented, but the BEHAVIOUR they show must come from the source.

TITLE (front of the card, and the card's permanent fingerprint)
  The title is shown as the large heading of the card. Directly above it the employee already sees a label that names the flow and form (the "context") and a badge with the module. So the title is ONLY the question or claim. Never start it with the form name, the module or a prefix such as "Material Receipt Note (GRN) - ". That repeats the label and wastes the heading.

  A later run sees only the item key, level, topic_key and this title when it decides whether a topic is already taught. So the title alone must tell a stranger exactly which fact, rule, formula or situation the card covers. Write every title as if it will be the only thing anyone reads.

  - Write one natural question or short claim an employee would say aloud. 12 to 110 characters; aim for 90 or fewer so it fits the card heading.
  - It must still name the document or term it is about, because the form name is no longer in it ("the receipt", "the gate entry", "the order", "an authorized document"). Never write "it" or "this form".
  - Name three things when they apply: (1) the exact thing, rule, term or document (2) the condition or situation (3) the outcome or decision being asked about.
  - Start with the question word that matches the archetype, so the kind of card is visible: "What ... is" (definition), "Why ..." (rule and reason, misconception), "How ... is worked out" (formula), "Who ..." (roles), "What happens when ..." (cascade), "What should you check ..." (diagnosis), "... is it allowed, and what is the ...?" (scenario with figures).
  - Name the specific case, not the area. Put the distinguishing detail in the title (the status, the document, the quantity situation, the trigger).
  - Banned: generic or area-only titles such as "Overview", "Introduction", "Basics", "About", "Details", "Important rules", "How it works", "What to remember", "Common mistakes", "Tips".
  - Unique within its item across all levels, and not the same topic as any title of that item in EXISTING CARDS, even reworded. The push compares titles of the same item and REFUSES a new card whose title shares about 60 percent of its key words with a stored or new card, so a reworded repeat is rejected, not stored twice.
  - Never reword or "improve" an existing title. If a topic is taught, leave it.

  Title test (apply to every title):
    1. Cover the body and the label. Can someone tell exactly what the card teaches? If not, rewrite.
    2. Could this title also describe a different card on the same form? If yes, add the distinguishing detail.
    3. Would a later run, seeing only this title, avoid writing the same topic again? If not, rewrite.
    4. Does it start with the form name or module? If yes, remove it.

7. NO DUPLICATES: PROCEDURE YOU MUST FOLLOW
================================================================================
For every card you plan:
  0. If ASSIGNED SLOTS are given, write exactly those cards. The slots were chosen so they cannot repeat what is stored.
  1. List the angles and archetypes already used for that item and level in EXISTING CARDS.
  2. Pick an angle NOT yet used for that item and level.
  3. Compare your title with ALL existing titles of the same item, using the titles only. If an existing title already covers the same fact, rule, formula or situation, even in different words, discard your card and plan another. Do not try to guess what an existing body says; the title is the complete record of that card.
  4. Compare with the other new cards in this run. No two cards teach the same fact.
  5. Facts do not repeat across levels. A hard card builds on an easy fact; it does not restate it.
  6. If the source offers nothing new for an item, SKIP it and mention it in your reply message. Do not pad.
  7. The push refuses a new card whose title is too close to a stored or new title of the same item. A reworded repeat will be rejected, so do not try to slip one through.

8. AUDIENCE CONTRACT: FUNCTIONAL ONLY
================================================================================
Write for the business user: Storekeepers, Buyers, Quality Inspectors, Accounts Payable clerks, Plant Supervisors, Approvers. Use clear business names for forms ("Purchase Order", "Material Receipt Note (GRN)", "Gate Entry").

STRICTLY FORBIDDEN in titles and bodies (an automatic leak check rejects them):
  - Form codes (fPurchaseOrder, mXxx, tXxx), camelCase or snake_case identifiers.
  - Table, view or schema names. Any word of the form Name.Name with no space after the full stop.
  - Words: table, column, dataset, database, schema, query, SQL, stored procedure, method, API, backend, source code.
  - Screen mechanics: dropdown, text box, check box, radio button, grid, popup, tab, toolbar, button, combo, icon.
  - Internal rule or message ids (BR-05, MSG-12, VAL-3) and status or validation code numbers.
  - Field lengths or formats ("(50)", "max 1000 characters", "10 digits").
  - Keyboard shortcuts.
Business numbers are fine and encouraged: "within 2 percent of the order quantity", "after 30 days".

{

  "items": [

      "key": "<the item key exactly as in TARGET ITEMS>",

          "source_refs": ["forms/<file>.yaml", "rule:<key>"]

      ]
    }

}

Only these fields exist. Do not add any other field (no counts, no archetype, no skipped list).

Rules:
  - One entry per TARGET ITEM in "items". An item with no new cards is left out. Report skipped items and the reason in your reply message, not in the file.
  - "context" is the small label shown above every card. The module name is already shown next to it on every card, so do NOT start the context with the module. Use "<Flow> > <Form>", for example "Procure-to-Pay > Material Receipt Note (GRN)". For a cross-form card use the pattern name, for example "Common patterns > Transaction forms". Keep the same context text for every run of the same item; changing it later creates a new version of every card.
  - "title" at item level is the business name of the form, flow or module. It is used as the item's name when the item is new. It is NOT repeated inside the card titles.
  - "scope" and "key" decide which item the cards belong to. Use scope "form" with the form key, scope "flow" with a process key or a common key (section 3B), scope "module" with the module code. Use a key that is not already used by an item of another type.
  - "source_refs" lists the knowledgegraph files or rule keys the card was written from. At least one per card. For a cross-form card list the example forms it was checked against.
