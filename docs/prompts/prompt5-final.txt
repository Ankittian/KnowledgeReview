1. LEARNING FIRST: HOW A LEARNING CARD DIFFERS FROM A TEST QUESTION
================================================================================
Before writing a card, finish this sentence: "After reading this, the employee can ______." If you cannot, do not write the card.

Rules that make a card teach instead of test:
  1. ANSWER FIRST. The first line of the body is a one-sentence answer. The rest explains it in plain sentences in time order, with no part labels (section 1B). Never make the reader guess before they learn.
  2. TERM FIRST. Every business term (free-of-cost quantity, challan, tolerance, drop-shipment, Release status) is defined in plain words the first time it appears on the card. A new employee must be able to read the card cold. If a card only makes sense after another card, rewrite it or drop it.
  3. SELF-CONTAINED SCENARIOS. Put every number and condition the reader needs inside the card. Never write "the tolerance warning from before" or "as taught earlier".
  4. WHY, NOT JUST WHAT. Every card gives the business reason behind the rule or step, but only a reason the KB supports (see the KB-ONLY RULE). No source reason, no reason line.
  5. BEHAVIOUR, NOT LABELS. Teach what the system does and what the user should do. Never teach an internal rule id, message id, code number or column name. Teach the behaviour behind it. ("An authorized receipt cannot be saved again" is the lesson, not the rule's number.)
  6. NO EXAM WORDING in the body: no "Question:", "Answer:", "Name three", "True or false", "Which of the following".
  7. ONE IDEA PER CARD. If a card needs "and also", split it.

The title is the front of the card: a question or claim the employee can self-check ("Do I know this?"). The body is the back: the lesson.

1B. LEARNER-FIRST, PROCESS-DRIVEN WRITING IN PLAIN SENTENCES (NO LABELS)
================================================================================
Teach the process in time order, but write it as plain, flowing sentences. NO part labels in the body: never write "Why:", "What:", "Who:", "How:" or "Transaction flow:".
The reader is a fresher with no ERP or domain knowledge. Do not summarise roles. Explain how the work really happens, in the order it happens: the business need, what the thing is, who does it, how it is done step by step, and which ERP documents record it. Every card must connect PEOPLE, ACTIVITIES and ERP TRANSACTIONS.

What every card covers (as ideas, in this order, never as labels)
  1. The answer and what the thing is: the lead line. Define every new term here, at first use.
  2. The business need: why it matters, in a natural sentence. Only a reason the KB supports.
  3. The people: who does it, who hands the work to them, who receives it next. Use the role names the KB uses.
  4. The activity in time order: what the person does, what the system does in response, what triggers the next step.
  5. The documents: the ERP documents raised in order, the status each moves through, and what each changes. Write this as plain sentences. Do NOT end the card with an arrow chain of documents. Mention the documents before or after only when the card is about their order, and use business names only.

Rules
  - CHRONOLOGICAL. The earlier event comes before the later one.
  - PEOPLE + ACTIVITY + TRANSACTION TOGETHER. Each sentence or step says who acts, what they do, and which document or status results. Fold the reason and the role into the same sentence instead of giving each its own line. Example: "The storekeeper counts the goods against the supplier's delivery paper, so the company pays only for what arrived."
  - NO CLOSING CHAIN. Do not end a card with a line of documents joined by "->". If the card is about the order of documents or statuses, say it in a plain sentence.
  - EXPLAIN, DO NOT SUMMARISE. ORDINARY WORDS. No unexplained jargon.
  - KB-ONLY STILL APPLIES. If the KB gives no reason, role or document link, leave it out. Never invent it. Report thin cases in your reply message.
  - NO SCREEN MECHANICS. Describe business actions, never clicks or controls.

Depth by level (short cards)
  - EASY: lead line, then one or two plain sentences (need and people).
  - MEDIUM: lead line, two or three plain sentences or 2-3 "- " steps.
  - HARD: lead line (the answer), a sentence of need and people, 2-4 "- " steps with real-looking numbers in time order, and one sentence on what to do if it goes wrong.
  - Special cases. Diagnosis card: the steps are the checks in order. Formula card: the steps are the calculation with numbers. Comparison card: the lead line states both items and a sentence states the difference.

3. QUESTION ARCHETYPES (WHAT KIND OF CARD TO WRITE)
================================================================================
Choose from these archetypes. Each maps to the fixed angle slugs in section 6. For each form, use at least four DIFFERENT archetypes, and where the source supports them include at least one misconception card (M7) and one scenario card (H1, H5). Questions that are not about one form use the common archetypes P1-P5 in section 3B.

Each archetype shows an example question for a sample form (the Material Receipt Note). They are patterns of question, not facts to copy. Find the equivalent question for YOUR form in its source material. If the source cannot support an archetype for that form, skip that archetype. Write every question as a full descriptive title (section 6): include the role, the document, the situation and the outcome. The short example questions below are patterns only.

EASY ARCHETYPES (what, who, when)
  E1  One-line definition: what the thing records or does, and what it is NOT.
      angle: purpose.   Example: "What does a Material Receipt Note record, and what does it not do?"
  E2  Look-alike terms told apart: two words people confuse, measured against different things.
      angle: vocabulary.   Example: "Partial receipt vs short receipt: which is which?" / "Purchase Order vs receipt: promise vs proof."
  E3  Where it sits in the flow: what comes before, what it hands over, what it feeds.
      angle: position-in-flow.   Example: "Why is there no direct link from a receipt to Accounts Payable? It goes through the Purchase Bill."
  E4  Term first: one business term that later cards rely on, defined with a plain example.
      angle: vocabulary.   Example: "What is free-of-cost quantity and why is it kept apart?" / "What is a drop-shipment receipt?" / "What does GR Suspense hold?"
  E5  Takeaway or common slip in one line: a mistake learners make, stated and corrected.
      angle: purpose or outputs.   Example: "Why must you never treat the challan quantity as the received quantity?"

MEDIUM ARCHETYPES (how and why)
  M1  Rule with its reason: the rule, the business reason, the user's fix.
      angle: standard-checks.   Example: "Why can an authorized receipt not be saved again?" / "Why must the source document be chosen first?"
  M2  Steps in order, with why the order matters.
      angle: sequence.   Example: "What are the steps to receive material against an order, and what happens on save?"
  M3  Formula with a small worked example.
      angle: limits-tolerances.   Example: "How are accepted, short and excess quantity worked out?" (include numbers)
  M4  Route A versus route B: what one path adds or locks compared with the other.
      angle: prerequisites or hand-offs.   Example: "What does receiving through a gate entry lock compared with receiving against an order?"
  M5  Status meaning: what each status allows and what moves it on.
      angle: status-lifecycle.   Example: "What can still be changed at each status, and what is locked after inspection?"
  M6  Who must do what: roles, approval order, what only the last approver can do.
      angle: approvals-roles.   Example: "Under multi-level approval, who can authorize and where?"
  M7  Misconception: "Why did X NOT happen?" or "Why is the common belief wrong?"
      angle: standard-checks or status-lifecycle.   Example: "Why did stock not rise when stores saved the receipt?" / "A form field looks compulsory on screen: is it really required?"

HARD ARCHETYPES (apply and consequences)
  H1  Numeric scenario: apply a rule to figures and state the decision and the result.
      angle: tolerance-calculation.   Example: "Order 2,000, tolerance 2 percent, three deliveries: is the third allowed and what is the excess?" / "Challan 1,000, counted 985, rejected 15: what goes to supplier, stores and accounts?"
  H2  Cascade: a change or event here, and every record it moves elsewhere.
      angle: cascade-effects or cross-module-impact.   Example: "What happens to other records when an authorized receipt is deleted?" / "What changes downstream when freight is added after the receipt?"
  H3  Diagnose: a symptom, the likely causes, the order to check them.
      angle: failure-diagnosis.   Example: "An authorized receipt shows no stock: what to check, in order." / "A reference does not appear in the list: likely causes."
  H4  Correction and reversal: what to do when a posted document is wrong.
      angle: reversal-correction.   Example: "A wrong receipt is authorized and part of its stock is already used: what are the options?"
  H5  Case walkthrough: one realistic case, step by step, through several controls.
      angle: scenario-walkthrough.   Example: "Three deliveries of steel plate against one order, with inspection and a month-end in between."
  H6  Conflicting controls or exceptions: two checks that seem to disagree.
      angle: exceptions.   Example: "The user accepts a warning but the save is still refused: why?"

ARCHETYPES THAT MUST NOT BECOME CARDS (they test memory, they do not teach)
  X1  Identifier lookup: "Which message appears when...", "Which column holds...", "Which validation number...", "About how many forms use...". Teach the behaviour, never the label.
  X2  Keyboard shortcuts and hidden keys.
  X3  Technical structure for a business audience: table names, column names, procedure order, save-transaction internals.
  X4  Long list recall: "List all seven causes". Write one card per cause, or a diagnosis card (H3) that gives the checks in order.
  X5  Open points: anything the source marks as not documented, inferred, or to be confirmed. Skip and mention it in your reply message.
  X6  Questions that need another card or session to make sense.

3B. COMMON (CROSS-FORM) CARDS: QUESTIONS THAT BELONG TO A FLOW OR THE MODULE
================================================================================
Some questions are not about one form. They are true across several forms, or about a flow, a hand-off, a business idea or the whole module. Write these as COMMON cards. They sit under a flow or module item instead of a form item, and the Browse screen shows them under their own "Common questions" button, separate from "Form questions".

Use a common card when the same answer holds for several forms (a shared rule), when you compare two forms, when the question is about the order of documents in a flow, or when the question is about one business idea used in many places.

Common archetypes (each follows the level rules of section 2; the level shown is the usual one)
  P1  Shared rule: a rule that holds across several forms, with the reason.   medium   angle: standard-checks or approvals-roles
      Example: "Why is an authorized document locked, and how is it changed?" / "Why is a master record made inactive instead of deleted once it is used?"
  P2  Two forms compared: what each one records and who owns it.   easy or medium   angle: vocabulary or position-in-flow
      Example: "What is the difference between a gate entry and a material receipt?"
  P3  Hand-off chain: what each step needs from the one before and what blocks the next.   medium   angle: hand-offs or sequence
      Example: "What must exist before a purchase bill can be passed, and where does it come from?"
  P4  Cross-form scenario: one case followed through several documents.   hard   angle: scenario-walkthrough or cascade-effects
      Example: "A supplier delivers short: what happens at receipt, to the bill and to the order?"
  P5  Business idea used in many places: define it once, show where it applies.   easy or medium   angle: vocabulary or purpose
      Example: "What is tolerance, and which documents check it?"

How to write them safely
  - EVIDENCE. State a pattern only if the source shows it on at least three of the forms you name (two for a comparison of two forms). Never generalise from one form. If the forms differ, write the difference (P2) or skip the card.
  - SOURCE_REFS. List the form nodes the pattern was checked against, each as the node name, then its graph element id in square brackets, then its short node id in round brackets, for example "Purchase Order [4:478dbad0-cd96-4aa4-bac7-6d61036ab7b0:61142] (form:fPurchaseOrder)" (see section 9 for how to look the id up). They record where the pattern was checked and link the card to those forms in the knowledge base.
  - NAMES. Use business names only, never form codes (the leak check rejects them).
  - ITEMS. Use scope "flow" for patterns, flows and business ideas, and scope "module" for a module overview (key = the module code, for example MM). For a flow that already has a process file, use that process key (for example procure-to-pay). For a pattern that has no file, make a key from the module code, the topic and the word common, in lowercase with hyphens: mm-transaction-forms-common, mm-master-forms-common, mm-lot-concept-common. Item title: the business name, for example "Transaction Forms: Common Questions". Context: "Common patterns > Transaction forms".
  - MIX. About 70 percent form cards, 15 percent common cards, 15 percent flow and module cards, unless TARGET ITEMS says otherwise.
  - Common cards count in the ledger like any other: compare their titles with EXISTING CARDS of the same item.

CARD E1
level: easy
topic_key: purpose--what-a-receipt-records
title: What does a Material Receipt Note record when a supplier delivers goods, and what does it not record?
body:
  A **Material Receipt Note** records what arrived from a supplier and how much was accepted. It is not the supplier's bill.
  The company needs proof of receipt before it pays, so the **storekeeper** records it when the goods are delivered.

CARD E4
level: easy
topic_key: vocabulary--free-of-cost-quantity
title: What is free-of-cost quantity on a receipt, and why is it kept apart from the paid quantity?
body:
  **Free-of-cost quantity** is material a supplier sends without charge, such as a sample.
  The storekeeper enters it on the receipt, so it enters stock but is not billed.
  Material Receipt Note (free quantity) -> stock up -> Purchase Bill (not billed)

CARD M1
level: medium
topic_key: standard-checks--authorized-receipt-is-frozen
title: Why can the storekeeper not change a Material Receipt Note again once it has been authorized?
body:
  An **authorized** receipt cannot be saved again, because its stock is already live.
  Other documents may depend on that stock, so a silent edit could change issued or billed stock.
  The storekeeper saves the receipt as **Initial** or **Release**, which posts nothing. The approver then authorizes it, and the accepted quantity posts to stock in that same save.
  Material Receipt Note (Initial -> Release -> Authorized) -> stock -> issues and Purchase Bill
----
CARD M2
level: medium
topic_key: sequence--receiving-against-an-order
title: In what order do the storekeeper, quality inspector and approver handle a delivery against a purchase order?
body:
  A delivery is handled in time order: count, inspect, authorize, then stock rises, so goods are not usable stock until they are checked.
  - The storekeeper enters the counted quantity against the order and saves as **Release**.
  - The quality inspector records the inspection result.
  - The approver authorizes, and the accepted quantity joins stock.
  Purchase Order -> Material Receipt Note (Release) -> Inspection -> Material Receipt Note (Authorized) -> stock
----
CARD M3
level: medium
topic_key: limits-tolerances--accepted-short-excess-formulas
title: How are accepted, short and excess quantity worked out when a storekeeper enters a received quantity?
body:
  The receipt works out three figures from the counted quantities. Short drives the supplier memo, excess drives the order limit check, accepted drives stock and the bill.
  - **Accepted** = received minus rejected minus free (free = sent without charge). Example: 300 - 0 - 20 = 280.
  - **Short** = challan (the supplier's own count) minus received.
  - **Excess** = accepted plus earlier receipts plus free, minus order.
  Accepted -> stock and bill. Short -> supplier memo. Excess -> order limit check.
----
CARD M7
level: medium
topic_key: status-lifecycle--saved-receipt-adds-no-stock
title: Why did stock not rise after the storekeeper saved the receipt, and when does it actually rise?
body:
  Saving a receipt adds no stock. Stock rises only when the receipt is **Authorized**.
  Quality staff and approvers must check the material before it counts as usable. **Initial** and **Release** post nothing, and the approver's authorization adds the accepted quantity to stock in that save.
  Pending inspection or multi-level approval blocks authorization.
  Material Receipt Note (Initial -> Release -> Authorized) -> stock
----
CARD H1
level: hard
topic_key: tolerance-calculation--third-delivery-against-one-order
title: Is a third delivery of 540 units allowed on a 2,000-unit order with 2% tolerance, and what is the excess?
body:
  Yes: the total of 2,030 is under the limit of 2,040, so the excess is 30 units.
  Deliveries on one order are added together, so the company never accepts more than the order plus the agreed **tolerance** (extra percentage allowed). The storekeeper enters the delivery and the system checks it.
  - Earlier receipts 800 + 690 = 1,490, plus this delivery of 540, make 2,030.
  - The limit is 2,000 plus 2% = 2,040, so the receipt saves.
  - Excess = 2,030 minus 2,000 = 30.
  Purchase Order (2,000) -> Receipts 800, 690, 540 -> stock and Purchase Bill
  At 560 the total is 2,050 and the save is refused, even after Yes. Receive 550 or ask the buyer to amend the order.
----
CARD H3
level: hard
topic_key: failure-diagnosis--authorized-receipt-shows-no-stock
title: An authorized receipt shows no stock in the report: what should the storekeeper check, and in what order?
body:
  Usually nothing is wrong: the stock is held where the report is not looking. The storekeeper checks first and the support team takes over if nothing explains it.
  - A drop-shipment receipt (goods go straight to the customer) posts no stock.
  - If everything was rejected, accepted is zero and rejected stock is kept apart.
  - Accepted stock may be reserved for a request, so it is not free.
  - The stock may sit in another warehouse than the report covers.
  Material Receipt Note (Authorized) -> stock by warehouse, reserved, rejected -> report
  If none of these explains it, send the receipt number to the support team.

level: medium
topic_key: standard-checks--authorized-document-is-locked
title: Why is an authorized document in Material Management locked, and how is it changed afterwards?

  Stock, bills and other documents depend on them, so a silent edit would break the links. The last approver authorizes, and the buyer raises an **amendment** to change an order, approved again.

5. ANSWER FORMAT (the "body")
================================================================================
The body is read on the back of the card. It must be SHORT, ordered and easy to scan: a good colleague giving a new joiner the process in a few clear lines, not an essay. It follows section 1B, written as plain sentences. THE BODY HAS NO LABELS.

BODY SHAPE (in this order)
  1. Lead line: one sentence that answers the title and defines any new term. It carries the "what".
  2. Plain sentences (one or two) that give the business need and the people: who acts, who hands over, who receives next. Only reasons and roles the KB supports.
  3. The activity: one sentence when there is one action. When there are two or more actions, use "- " lines in time order, each naming who acts, what they do and which document or status results. Diagnosis: the checks in order. Formula: the calculation with the numbers in it.
  4. No closing chain. The body never ends with a line of documents joined by "->".
  5. At most ONE extra plain sentence, only for a real need: what to do if it goes wrong, or a real common slip. No label before it.

  - Never write "Why:", "What:", "Who:", "How:", "Transaction flow:", "If it goes wrong:" or "Watch out:". The text must read naturally without them.
  - A part the KB cannot support is left out, never filled with a guess.

LENGTH CAPS (strict)
  - Lead line: at most 20 words.
  - Each plain sentence line and the closing sentence: at most 25 words.
  - Each "- " step: at most 15 words.
  - Steps: at most 3 in a medium card and 4 in a hard card.
  - Say each fact once. Do not repeat the title, the lead line or an earlier line. Do not give a reason twice.
  - Cut filler: "in order to", "it is important that", "this means that", "in other words". Short sentences, one idea per line.
  - If a card is over its limit, delete the least useful line. Do not shorten by dropping the numbers or the roles.

BY LEVEL
  EASY   (2-3 lines): lead line, one or two plain sentences.
  MEDIUM (3-6 lines): lead line, two or three plain sentences or 2-3 steps. Optional one extra sentence.
  HARD   (6-9 lines): lead line (the answer or decision), a sentence of need and people, 2-4 steps with real-looking numbers in time order, and one sentence on what to do if it goes wrong.

CLARITY CHECKS for each line: one idea; plain words a fresher understands; the actor is named (the storekeeper, the approver, the system); the result is stated (what changes, what is blocked, what the user does next); no vague phrases ("various checks", "certain conditions", "as needed", "etc."). If a rule has conditions, name them.

Formatting rules:
  - Lines are separated by "\n" in the JSON string. One idea per line.
  - **Bold** (double asterisks) marks key business terms: roles, document names, statuses, limits, outcomes. Use 2-5 bold terms per card. Each bold phrase is 1-4 words. Never bold a whole sentence. Never nest bold. Every ** closes on the same line.
  - Steps start the line with "- " (dash and one space). No nested bullets. No "Step 1:" labels; the order of the lines is the order of the steps.
  - The arrow sign "->" is allowed only inside a plain sentence to show a status order, for example Initial -> Release -> Authorized. Never on a line of its own.
  - No headings (#), tables, links, emojis, italic markers or code formatting.
  - Plain sentences otherwise. Active voice, present tense. A SPACE after every full stop.

Numbers in worked examples may be invented, but the BEHAVIOUR, the roles, the statuses and the transaction order they show must come from the source.

6. TITLES AND KEYS: BUILT SO THE JOB NEVER DUPLICATES
================================================================================
TITLE (front of the card, and the card's permanent fingerprint)
  The title is shown as the large heading of the card. Directly above it the employee already sees a label that names the flow and form (the "context") and a badge with the module. So the title is ONLY the question or claim. Never start it with the form name, the module or a prefix such as "Material Receipt Note (GRN) - ". That repeats the label and wastes the heading.
