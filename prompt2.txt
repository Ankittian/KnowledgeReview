You are an expert ERP Knowledge Engineer and Technical Educator. Your task is to generate high-quality educational flashcards for the FUNCTIONAL AUDIENCE ONLY, directly from the raw ERP Knowledge Base in the `kb/` folder.

This is prompt2 (v2 Strategy): GENERALIZED DIFFICULTY & COMPREHENSIVE FORM COVERAGE.

================================================================================
1. CORE STRATEGY: GENERALIZED DIFFICULTY (SCOPE != DIFFICULTY)
================================================================================
Previously, difficulty was rigidly coupled to scope (module = easy, flow = medium, form = hard). This created a severe flaw: with dozens of forms in a module, hard questions vastly outnumbered easy and medium questions, making learning unbalanced and overwhelming.

Under this revised strategy, difficulty is a matter of DEPTH, and each level has a default source emphasis. These are guidelines, not fixed rules: you may draw from any scope when the content calls for it, as long as the depth matches the level.
- EASY: mostly MODULE-level cards (what the module governs), BUSINESS CONCEPT cards (key terms and what they mean), and FORMS OVERVIEW cards (what a screen is for and who uses it). Keep the focus on purpose, roles and terminology.
- MEDIUM: mostly the BUSINESS RULE PROCESS (standard document sequences, hand-offs between stages, everyday validations), plus medium-depth MODULE-level questions (how the module's processes connect). Keep the focus on how work normally moves.
- HARD: mostly FORMS (the detailed rules, locks, cascades and edge cases of individual screens), plus some of the harder BUSINESS RULE PROCESS and MODULE-level questions (cross-process governance, reconciliations, period controls). Keep the focus on depth and exceptions.

Difficulty is defined by CONCEPTUAL DEPTH, COGNITIVE DEMAND, and COMPLEXITY OF BUSINESS RULES:

| Level | Weight | Cognitive Depth & Focus | Length & Words |
|---|---|---|---|
| EASY | 1 | Operational Purpose & Roles: What a screen, flow, or module is for; who uses it; what commercial/operational need triggers it; basic terminology and prerequisites. | 3–5 lines | 25–75 words |
| MEDIUM | 3 | Standard Business Rules & Workflows: Standard document sequences; mandatory field requirements; stage-to-stage hand-offs; everyday operational validations; normal tolerances. | 5–9 lines | 80–180 words |
| HARD | 5 | Deep Governance, Edge Cases & Cascades: Multi-tier authorization hierarchies; terminal approval locks; strict tolerance formulas; rejected goods quarantine; cross-document status cascading; period lockouts; multi-currency reconciliations. | 8–14 lines | 140–280 words |

================================================================================
2. COMPREHENSIVE COVERAGE: ALL FORMS IN THE MODULE
================================================================================
Do NOT generate cards for only 2 or 3 forms. Curate cards for ALL active forms in the target module (MM has 87 forms spanning purchasing, inventory, gate control, quality inspection, material masters, and vendor masters), plus the module macro-overview and major flows.

Typical card allocation (adjust as the content requires):
- Standard / utility forms: 1 card (usually Easy, a Medium if it has real rules).
- Intermediate transaction / master forms: 1–2 cards (e.g. 1 Easy overview, 1 Medium rule card).
- Core transaction anchors (e.g. Purchase Order, Material Receipt Note, Indent, Gate Entry, RFQ, Quotation, CS, Material Issue, Inspection Entry): 2–3 cards, typically including a Hard card for their governance and edge cases.
- Module-level and business-concept cards: mostly Easy, with a few Medium where the cross-process logic is standard.
- Business-process cards: mostly Medium, with a few Hard for the complex governance paths.

This ensures a smooth, well-balanced distribution across the entire module:
- ~35–45% Easy cards (mostly module, concept and forms-overview)
- ~35–45% Medium cards (mostly business-process rules)
- ~15–25% Hard cards (mostly forms, with some harder process and module cards)
These percentages are targets. If the source material supports a different split without losing depth, prefer the better-fitting split and keep the level-to-source emphasis above.

================================================================================
3. AUDIENCE CONTRACT: FUNCTIONAL ONLY
================================================================================
Every flashcard must be written strictly for the business user (Storekeepers, Buyers, Quality Inspectors, Accounts Payable, Plant Supervisors).

Display titles only: Use clear business names (e.g. "Purchase Order", "Material Receipt Note (GRN)", "Gate Entry", "Request for Quotation").

STRICTLY FORBIDDEN IN FUNCTIONAL CARDS:
- NO form codes (fPurchaseOrder, fGRN, fIndent, etc.).
- NO table, view, or schema names (Purchase.PMPurchaseOrder, Inventory.IGRNMain, etc.).
- NO database/programming terms (stored procedure, sp_, SQL, query, method, API, backend, column, dataset).
- NO UI mechanics (dropdown, text box, check box, grid, popup, tab, button, icon, toolbar).
- NO internal rule or message IDs (BR-05, MSG-12, etc.).
- NO field lengths or formats ("(50)", "max 1000 characters") or keyboard shortcuts (Ctrl+Y).

================================================================================
4. FLASHCARD VERIFICATION FORMAT
================================================================================
Each flashcard is an educational STATEMENT TO VERIFY (teaching how the ERP functions and confirming business rules):
- `topic_key`: Unique kebab-case slug within the form/scope.
- `title`: Concise topic title (8–110 characters, never ending in a question mark).
- `body`: Plain sentences separated by `\n` (no markdown asterisks, no bullets, no numbered lists).
- `source_refs`: Array of strings pointing to source sections or concepts (e.g. ["purpose", "validations", "rule:approved-vendor"]).
